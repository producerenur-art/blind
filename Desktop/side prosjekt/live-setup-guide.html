Go live on SiriusFM Mac + Traktor verified 20 Sep 2026

A setup guide for Mac, Windows PC and any DJ gear — controller, mixer, CDJs or just software.

SiriusFM's live broadcast runs on WebRTC straight from your browser — there is no separate broadcasting app (BUTT, OBS, Icecast) to install. The browser takes your mixed master signal as an audio input, just like a microphone, and sends it to listeners in real time. So every setup comes down to one thing: get your DJ mix into the computer as an audio input, then pick that input on the live page.

Which route is yours?

Your gearRoute
DJ software on a Mac (Traktor, Serato, rekordbox, VirtualDJ, Mixxx …), with or without a controllerA. Mac — software loopback (verified with Traktor)
DJ software on a Windows PC, with or without a controllerB. Windows PC — software loopback
A mixer with a USB sound card (Pioneer DJM, Allen & Heath Xone, Rane, Denon Prime …)C. Hardware — the mixer is the input
CDJs / standalone players / turntables / hardware synths through any mixerC. Hardware — audio interface line-in

Routes A and B only copy your computer's audio. Route C sends the mixer's own master output, so it works with any source that goes through the mixer.

Before you start (all routes)

  1. Use Chrome (or another Chromium browser like Edge). The page must be https://www.siriusfm.no.
  2. Log in and request your slot with Apply to go live in the More menu. You can start about 10 minutes before your approved time. Trusted broadcasters only pick a time.
  3. Close everything you do not need (browser tabs, other music apps, video calls). A busy computer is the most common cause of stuttering.
  4. Use a cable connection to your router if you can. Wi-Fi works but is less stable for a long set.
  5. Turn off sound effects and notifications on your computer so they never end up in the broadcast.
  6. Turn off power saving and plug in the charger. See Power saving below.

A. Mac — software loopback

Verified end-to-end with Traktor Pro 4 + Traktor Kontrol S2 MK2. Other DJ software works the same way: send its master output to the Multi-Output Device.

One-time setup

  1. Install BlackHole 2ch (free) from existential.audio/blackhole.
  2. Open Audio MIDI Setup → click + → Create Multi-Output Device.
  3. Tick both your controller / headphone output (or your Mac speakers) and BlackHole 2ch.
  4. Set the sample rate to 44.1 kHz on the Multi-Output Device. A mismatch can give silent or distorted sound.

Every time you go live

  1. System Settings → Sound → Output → Multi-Output Device. Make sure mute is not ticked.
  2. In your DJ software, set the audio device / master output to the Multi-Output Device. In Traktor: Preferences → Audio Setup → Audio Device, and Output Routing → Mixing Mode Internal, Output Master L/R = Multi-Output Device L/R.
  3. Play something and confirm you can hear it yourself.
  4. On SiriusFM click Grant access, pick BlackHole 2ch under Audio input, then Go live.
  5. Check that Sent (L)/(R) shows real dB values (for example −20 dB), not −∞ dB.

B. Windows PC — software loopback

This is the standard Windows method. The SiriusFM team has not tested it on every setup, so run the dB-meter check below before your real set.

One-time setup

  1. Install VB-CABLE Virtual Audio Cable (free) from vb-audio.com/Cable, then restart the PC. It adds a virtual device called CABLE Input (playback) and CABLE Output (recording).
  2. Prefer more control? Voicemeeter (also free) can send the same mix to the broadcast and to your headphones at the same time.

Every time you go live

  1. In your DJ software, set the master output to CABLE Input (VB-Audio Virtual Cable). Use the WASAPI or DirectSound device type if your software offers it. Some programs only use ASIO for their own controller, and then you need Voicemeeter to bridge it.
  2. To hear yourself, either send the headphone / cue output to your controller's sound card, or in Windows Sound settings → Recording → CABLE Output → Properties → Listen → tick Listen to this device and choose your speakers (this adds a small delay).
  3. On SiriusFM click Grant access, pick CABLE Output (VB-Audio Virtual Cable) under Audio input, then Go live.
  4. Check that Sent (L)/(R) shows real dB values, not −∞ dB.

C. Hardware — the mixer or interface is the input

Works on both Mac and PC, and needs no virtual cable. It is also the route for CDJs, turntables and standalone gear.

  1. Mixer with a USB sound card (for example Pioneer DJM, Allen & Heath Xone, Rane, Denon Prime): connect it by USB and open its settings utility. Choose the master (or record) signal as the USB output. The computer now sees the mixer as an audio input.
  2. Any other mixer or controller: take the REC OUT or MASTER OUT and plug it into the line input of a USB audio interface (Focusrite, Behringer UCA202, Audient and similar). Use the line input, not the microphone input.
  3. On SiriusFM click Grant access, pick the mixer or interface under Audio input, then Go live.
  4. Set the level so the loudest peaks stay clearly below clipping, around −6 dB on the Sent (L)/(R) meter. If it is clipping, lower the interface gain, not just the volume in your player.

Power saving — keep Chrome and your computer awake

A long set can be cut off or turn choppy if the computer saves power. Do this before every broadcast.

In Chrome

  1. Open Settings → Performance.
  2. Turn Memory Saver off, or keep it on and add www.siriusfm.no under Always keep these sites active. Memory Saver can put a background tab to sleep, and a sleeping tab stops sending your sound.
  3. Turn Energy Saver off for the broadcast. It slows down tabs and video when the battery is low or the laptop is unplugged.
  4. Keep the SiriusFM tab open in its own window. Do not minimise it, and do not put another window in full screen on top of it.

On a Mac

  1. Plug in the power adapter. A set on battery power is the most common reason for dropouts.
  2. System Settings → Battery → turn Low Power Mode off.
  3. Make sure the Mac does not sleep: in System Settings → Lock Screen, set Turn display off on power adapter to a long time, and in Battery → Options tick Prevent automatic sleeping on power adapter when the display is off.
  4. Quick alternative: open Terminal and run caffeinate -dims. The Mac stays awake until you close the window or press Ctrl+C.

On a Windows PC

  1. Plug in the charger.
  2. Settings → System → Power & battery: set Power mode to Best performance and turn Battery saver off.
  3. Under Screen, sleep & hibernate timeouts set Sleep to Never while plugged in. The screen may turn off, but the computer must not sleep.

Remember to put your settings back to normal after the set if you want to save battery again.

Where to set the master output in popular DJ software

Menu names change between versions. Look for the audio or sound settings and set the master / main output to the virtual device (routes A and B), or leave it on your hardware (route C).

⚠ Pitfalls

  • Never switch your audio device while you are live. It breaks capture silently: the page still says LIVE but the sound is gone. Stop the broadcast (or reload the page) first.
  • Mac: reinstalling BlackHole breaks the Multi-Output Device. Delete it and build it again.
  • No browser sound processing. SiriusFM already turns off echo cancellation, noise suppression and auto gain. Do not add browser extensions that change the microphone.
  • Mute other SiriusFM tabs and the SiriusFM player while you broadcast. Otherwise you hear an echo.

Troubleshooting

SymptomLikely cause
Sent (L)/(R) shows −∞ dBWrong Audio input chosen, the virtual device is muted (Mac), or your DJ software is not sending to the virtual device or mixer
You hear nothing, but the DJ software's meters moveThe software output is not set to the Multi-Output Device (Mac), or the headphone output is not set (PC)
CABLE or BlackHole is missing from the input listRestart the computer after installing. Then click Grant access again and allow the microphone in the browser
Sound is pumped or sounds artificialBrowser sound processing is on, or the level is clipping. Lower the gain
Stuttering or dropoutsOverloaded computer or weak connection. Close other programs, use a cable, and try a larger audio buffer in the DJ software
Sound stops or turns choppy after a while, especially when the SiriusFM tab is in the background or the laptop is on batteryChrome Memory Saver or Energy Saver, Low Power Mode, or sleep. See Power saving
It goes silent halfway throughThe audio device was changed while live. Stop and start the broadcast again
You hear an echoAnother SiriusFM tab or the player is still playing. Mute it
